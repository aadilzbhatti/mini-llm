The lighthouse keeper woke before dawn every day. He climbed the stairs,
counted them out of habit, and reached the lamp room at the top. The lamp
turned slowly through the fog. Ships passed in the dark and the keeper
watched them pass. He wrote the time of each ship in a small notebook.

In the notebook he wrote the weather too. Rain, fog, clear, rain again.
The pages filled up week after week. When a notebook was full he put it on
the shelf below the window and started a new one. The shelf held eleven
notebooks and there was room for more.

Boats came to the island twice a month with food and oil for the lamp.
The keeper carried the boxes up from the dock himself. He liked the walk.
On clear days he could see the mainland from the path, a thin grey line
between the water and the sky, and he would stop and look at it for a while
before going inside.

At night the lamp turned and the fog came in and the ships passed and the
keeper wrote the time of each one in his notebook. That was the work. It
was the same every day, and he did not mind that it was the same, because
the light had to turn and someone had to be there to turn it.
